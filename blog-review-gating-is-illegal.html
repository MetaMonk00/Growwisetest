Filtering Out Bad Reviews Is Illegal — Do This Instead

5 min read · GrowWise Team

Some marketing companies sell a trick that sounds smart. Before asking for a review, they survey the customer. Happy? Here is the Google link. Unhappy? You get sent to a private form nobody ever sees.

It is called review gating. It is not a smart trick. It is against the law, it breaks Google's rules, and it puts the name you spent years building at risk.

What the rules say

In the US, the FTC has a rule about consumer reviews. It bans hiding bad reviews to make a business look better than it is. The FTC has gone after companies for doing exactly that. On top of that, Google's own rules ban review gating. Asking only the people you think will say something nice is not allowed. Google can pull your reviews or punish your page for it.

Here is the ugly part. If a marketing company set gating up for you, your page takes the hit. Not theirs.

It backfires even if nobody catches you

A page with nothing but perfect reviews looks fake to shoppers. People look for the bad ones on purpose to see if a business is real. A rough review you answered well often builds more trust than another five star rave. Gating also hides the only honest feedback you get about your own shop.

The legal way gets you more reviews anyway

Here is what gating companies miss. Most service businesses do not have a bad review problem. They have an asking problem. Fix the asking and the math takes care of itself.

  • Ask everyone, every time. No sorting. No picking. Same ask for every customer.
  • Ask at the right time. Right after the job, while it is fresh.
  • Make it one tap. A direct link to your review page. Every extra step costs you reviews.
  • Answer all of them. Especially the rough ones. A calm reply shows people how you treat folks.

Ask all of your customers and the good ones will bury the occasional three star. You end up with a page that is legal, safe, and easy to believe.
