Reputation

Filtering Out Bad Reviews Is Illegal — Do This Instead

5 min read · GrowWise Team

Plenty of marketing companies quietly sell a feature that sounds clever: before asking for a review, survey the customer. Rate you highly? Here's the Google link. Rate you poorly? You get routed to a private feedback form that never sees daylight.

It's called review gating, and it is not a clever growth hack. It is illegal, it violates Google's policies, and it puts the reputation you spent years building at risk.

What the rules actually say

In the United States, the FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials prohibits businesses from suppressing negative reviews to create a misleadingly positive picture — and the agency has brought enforcement actions against companies that did exactly that. Separately, Google's own review policies prohibit review gating: soliciting reviews selectively from customers you expect to be positive is against the rules, and Google can remove reviews or penalize the profile.

The uncomfortable part: if a marketing company set gating up for you, your business profile carries the risk — not theirs.

Why it backfires even when nobody catches you

A profile with nothing but flawless five-star reviews reads as fake to modern customers. Shoppers actively look for critical reviews to judge whether a business is real, and a well-handled negative review often builds more trust than another anonymous rave. Gating also robs you of the only honest signal you have about what is going wrong in your business.

The compliant approach that gets more reviews anyway

Here is the thing gating companies miss: most businesses don't have a bad-review problem, they have an asking problem. Fix the asking and the math takes care of itself.

  • Ask everyone, every time. No screening, no sorting. Every customer gets the same invitation.
  • Ask at the right moment. Right after the work is finished, while the experience is fresh.
  • Make it one tap. A direct link to your review page. Every extra step costs you reviews.
  • Respond to all of them. Especially the critical ones. A calm, professional reply is public proof of how you treat people.

Ask 100% of your customers honestly and you will bury the occasional three-star review under genuine praise — with a profile that is bulletproof, legal, and actually believable.
