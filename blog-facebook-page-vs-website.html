Is A Facebook Page Enough? Why Social Media Can't Replace A Website

4 min read · GrowWise Team

"I don't need a website — I have a Facebook page." It feels true: the page was free, it has followers, sometimes a post gets shared. But building your business on a social platform alone has a structural problem: you're building on rented ground.

The algorithm decides who sees you

On social media, a platform's algorithm chooses whether your posts reach anyone — and organic reach for business pages has famously dwindled. Your website is the opposite: when someone searches for your service, your site can meet them at the exact moment they're ready to buy. Search traffic is intent — people actively looking for what you sell — while social traffic is interruption.

You don't own the audience — or the account

Pages get restricted, hacked, and locked out every day, sometimes with no human to appeal to. Every follower, photo, and review on a platform belongs to the platform. Your website, your domain, and the leads it captures in your system are yours permanently.

Credibility works against you

Stanford's Web Credibility Research shows customers judge a business by the professionalism of its online presence. A business whose only home is a social page — next to memes and marketplace listings — reads as smaller and less established than one with a clean professional site.

The right answer: both, with the website as the hub

  • Website = the hub. It gets found on Google, captures leads with chat and forms, books appointments, and shows your reviews.
  • Social = a spoke. Great for visibility and staying top-of-mind — with every post pointing back to the hub you own.
Use social media to get attention. Use your website to turn that attention into booked appointments you own.
