Why The First Business To Respond Wins The Job

4 min read · GrowWise Team

Here's an uncomfortable truth about local business: the job usually doesn't go to the best company. It goes to the first company that responds.

This isn't a hunch — it's one of the most studied effects in sales. A well-known study published in Harvard Business Review found that companies contacting a new lead within an hour were nearly seven times more likely to have a meaningful conversation with the decision maker than those that waited even an hour longer. Related research from InsideSales.com found the odds of reaching a lead fall off a cliff after just five minutes.

Speed is a trust signal

Think about it from the customer's side. They reached out to three businesses. One texted back in under a minute. One called back the next morning. One never responded at all. Before anyone has quoted a price, the first business has already proven something the others haven't: they're on it.

You can't out-hustle this problem

Most owners try to solve response speed with effort — answering calls on ladders, replying to leads at dinner, feeling guilty about every voicemail. But you physically cannot answer every inquiry within five minutes while also doing the actual work. That's not a discipline problem; it's a systems problem.

The businesses winning your market aren't working harder than you. Their systems respond in seconds while they work — chat widgets that engage instantly, missed calls that text themselves back, booking pages that fill the calendar without a phone call.

What "always first" looks like

  • A chat widget on your website that captures the visitor the moment interest peaks.
  • Missed call text back so a voicemail becomes a live text conversation in seconds.
  • Online booking so ready-to-buy customers schedule themselves — instantly, even at midnight.
  • Automatic follow-up so the lead who "wants to think about it" hears from you again.

Put those together and you're first every time — without touching your phone.
